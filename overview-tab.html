Proposed Regulation Simulation: what is law, what is signaled, and which decisions carry the most weight

The component rule and the category bans for routers and inverters are already law. The Chinese optical transceiver curb exists only as press and analyst reporting. The decisions that matter most now are the ones that are hard to reverse: how to zone federal tenants, when to adopt the next optics generation, and how to source power equipment. How the weights work: probability, impact and lock-in

Weight = 100 × P(drivers bind) × (0.6 × Impact + 0.4 × Lock-in) ÷ 5

Probability

Each decision has drivers: the instruments that would force it. Each driver has a probability set by its evidence level and a relevance between 0 and 1 for that decision. The drivers combine as independent risks: P = 1 − Π(1 − p × r).

Impact (1–5)

The cost, schedule or legal exposure if the decision is wrong when the driver binds. A 5 means a fabric generation or a federal contract is at stake. A 1 means a single replaceable device.

Lock-in (1–5)

How hard the decision is to undo. Physical zoning and the optics architecture score 5. Contract clauses and records programs score 1. When a decision has low lock-in but high probability, it is marked no-regret.

These are teaching defaults, not forecasts. Counsel should replace them with their own view and record why. The simulation caps every non-enacted driver at amber in the part view, so a reported rule is never shown as law.
