Confirmed rules now in force

ENACTED
FCC 26-50 · component-level restriction

Effective October 13, 2026. A new FCC equipment authorization can be refused where a device contains a logic-bearing hardware component produced by a Covered List entity. Existing FCC grants remain valid; the rule is prospective and does not yet ban software or firmware.

COVERED LIST
Consumer-grade routers

Foreign-produced consumer routers are covered. Conditional approvals exist for named families; waivers and prior grants should be checked by exact model and FCC ID. Enterprise datacenter routers are not named by this category rule.

COVERED LIST
Foreign-produced power inverters

New foreign-produced power inverters cannot receive FCC authorization without Conditional Approval. Applications for approval must be submitted by January 1, 2028, but the underlying prohibition operates now.

FAR / NDAA
DoD 1260H and semiconductor procurement

Section 805 direct restrictions on 1260H entities began June 30, 2026; indirect restrictions begin June 30, 2027. Section 5949 semiconductor restrictions become effective December 23, 2027.

Reported or proposed — do not treat as law

REPORTED
Possible new Chinese optical-transceiver curb

Reuters reporting describes an FCC restriction on new models, possibly beginning at 3.2T, but no FCC text or proceeding is confirmed. A Morgan Stanley readout mentioned a possible 65% US-BOM exemption. Neither figure should be loaded as binding.

PROPOSED BILL
S.5548 · national security systems

Introduced September 24, 2026 and referred to committee. It would bar covered InnoLight and Eoptolink transceivers from federal NSS procurement after enactment, with a five-year transition described in the proposal. It is not effective and commercial datacenters are outside its direct scope.

FNPRM
Possible expansion beyond component hardware

The FCC is seeking comment on software/firmware coverage, HBOM/SBOM disclosure, and separate producer-based versus production-location-based categories. These ideas are not restrictions today.

Before entering the simulation

Four items define the baseline:
FCC 26-50; consumer-router category rules; power-inverter category rules; 1260H / 5949 procurement restrictions.
Two items remain uncertain:
The optical-transceiver restriction and S.5548. Keep both as scenario assumptions, not compliance findings.

What to collect for each BOM line

  • Exact MPN and FCC ID / grant date
  • Actual manufacturer, producer and ultimate parent
  • Production and shipment origin
  • Logic-bearing subcomponents
  • US BOM value and DSP / laser vendors
  • Deployment zone and build phase
  • Conditional Approval expiry, if applicable

Decision memoScenario snapshot · November 23, 2026

Regulatory exposure decision memo

Decision: Authorize 3.2T design standard and segregate federal network zones.

Summary

The reported transceiver curb remains WATCH-level because no FCC draft text or proceeding has been published. The existing component rule creates no exposure for the listed transceiver makers, but white-label switches require subcomponent review.

Recommended moves

  1. Lock 1.6T qualified SKUs for the near-term fabric.
  2. Qualify two US-content 3.2T alternatives.
  3. Segregate federal workloads behind a vetted supply chain.
